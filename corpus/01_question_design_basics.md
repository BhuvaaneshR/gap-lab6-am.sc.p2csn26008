# Question Design Basics

## What a stem is

The stem is the part of a multiple-choice item that poses the problem. A good
stem can be answered by a knowledgeable candidate without reading the options
at all. If the stem cannot stand alone, the item is testing reading speed
rather than the subject.

## Rules that survive contact with real candidates

1. Put the question in the stem, not in the options. "Which of the following
   is NOT..." forces the candidate to hold four negations at once.
2. Keep the options parallel in length and grammar. The longest option is
   correct far more often than chance in hand-written banks, and test-wise
   candidates know it.
3. Avoid "all of the above". A candidate who recognises two correct options
   can select it without evaluating the third.
4. One defensible answer. If two options are arguably correct, the item will
   be challenged and the challenge will be upheld.

## Distractors

A distractor exists to be chosen by a candidate who holds a specific
misconception. A distractor that nobody selects is dead weight: it makes the
item a three-option question with four options' worth of reading. Review
selection frequencies after every sitting and replace anything below five per
cent.

## Difficulty

Difficulty is a property of the item and the cohort together, not of the item
alone. An item with a facility value above 0.95 discriminates nothing. One
below 0.20 is usually broken rather than hard.
