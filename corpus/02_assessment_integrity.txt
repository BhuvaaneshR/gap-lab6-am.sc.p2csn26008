ASSESSMENT INTEGRITY IN UNPROCTORED EXAMINATIONS
Internal guidance note, revision 4

1. THE PROBLEM

An unproctored examination cannot prevent collusion. It can only make
collusion expensive, detectable, or pointless. Controls that assume the
candidate is alone in a room are not controls; they are hopes.

2. CONTROLS THAT WORK

Item banking at scale. If every candidate receives a different set of
questions drawn from a large bank, the value of sharing answers falls sharply.
The bank must be large enough that overlap between any two candidates is low,
which in practice means several hundred items per topic.

Time pressure applied honestly. A tight window reduces the opportunity to
consult, but it also penalises slow readers who know the material. Use it
sparingly and state it in advance.

Question rotation. Items that have been sitting in a bank for three years are
in circulation. Treat an item's age as a risk factor.

3. CONTROLS THAT DO NOT WORK

Honour statements. Measurable effect close to zero in every published study.

Browser lockdown on a personal device. Defeated by a second device.

Webcam monitoring without a human watching. Produces evidence after the fact,
not prevention, and generates a volume of flags nobody reviews.

4. WHAT TO RECORD

Every item's exposure count. Every challenge and its outcome. Every change to
the bank, with a date and a reason.
